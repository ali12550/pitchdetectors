Best Audiobook Apps Compared (2026): Price, Library Size & Features

Pitch Detectors Editorial Team
•
8 min read
•
Updated October 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Audible remains the best overall choice for heavy listeners thanks to its credit system, massive exclusive catalog, and true ownership of titles.
  • Spotify is the best value for casual listeners who already pay for Premium, with a monthly allowance of audiobook listening hours included.
  • Libby is completely free with a library card and unbeatable on price, though popular titles often come with wait lists.
  • There is no single "best" app — the right choice depends on how many books you finish per month and whether you want to own or borrow.

Introduction: Why Choosing the Right Audiobook App Matters

A decade ago, choosing an audiobook app was simple: you bought audiobooks from Audible or you did not listen to audiobooks at all. Today the landscape is crowded. Audible still dominates, but Spotify has muscled in with bundled listening hours, Libby gives away millions of titles for free through public libraries, Apple Books and Google Play Books sell titles a la carte, Kobo Plus offers an all-you-can-listen subscription, and Chirp sells premium titles at deep daily discounts.

The wrong choice can easily cost you $100 or more per year in overpriced credits, unused subscriptions, or books you cannot keep. The right choice, matched to your actual listening habits, can cut your cost per book dramatically — sometimes to zero. In this guide we compare the seven most important audiobook apps on the factors that genuinely matter: pricing model, catalog depth, offline listening, playback-speed control, and who each app is truly best for. We scored every app on the same seven practical criteria — pricing model, catalog depth, ownership, offline listening, speed control, sleep timer and bookmarks, and discovery — and prices below are approximate since they change frequently.

Audible: Best Overall for Heavy Listeners

Audible's Premium Plus plan (around $14.95 per month in the US) gives you one credit per month, and each credit buys any audiobook regardless of its list price — including $40, 30-hour epics. Unused credits roll over for up to 12 months, and you own every title you buy with credits forever, even if you cancel. A cheaper Plus-only tier offers unlimited listening from a smaller rotating catalog without credits.

Where Audible truly pulls ahead is catalog depth and exclusives: the largest English-language audiobook library by a wide margin, including Audible Originals you cannot get anywhere else. Speed adjusts from 0.5x to 3.5x, Whispersync syncs your place with the Kindle edition, and offline downloads are generous. The main weakness is price for light listeners — one short book every two months makes $14.95 per credit poor value.

Spotify: Best for Casual Listeners Who Already Pay for Music

Spotify Premium (around $11.99 per month for an individual plan) bundles roughly 15 hours of audiobook listening per month into the music subscription. Fifteen hours covers about one average-length novel, or two shorter ones. When you run out, you can buy top-up hours, though topping up repeatedly gets expensive fast.

Spotify's pitch is convenience: one app and one subscription for music, podcasts, and books, with a genuinely good recommendation engine. The drawbacks are structural: you never own anything, the audiobook catalog is smaller than Audible's, and 15 hours is a hard ceiling heavy listeners will exhaust in the first week of the month.

Libby: The Best Free Option, Period

Libby, by OverDrive, connects to your public library card and lets you borrow ebooks and audiobooks for free — the same collection your local library buys, delivered to your phone. Loans typically last 7 to 21 days, downloads work offline, speed control runs from 0.6x to 3x, and the sleep timer is excellent.

The catch is availability. Popular new releases often have wait lists stretching weeks or months, and each library's catalog differs based on its budget. But for patient listeners, Libby's cost per book is literally zero, which no paid app can beat. Pro tip: many library systems let non-residents buy an annual card for a modest fee, and some readers hold cards from two or three systems to cut wait times. If you have not checked what your library offers lately, do it before paying for anything.

Other Notable Apps: Apple Books, Kobo Plus, Google Play Books & Chirp

Apple Books sells audiobooks individually with no subscription — you pay per title and own it permanently, with regular sales and flawless iOS integration (Siri, CarPlay, Apple Watch). It suits Apple users who buy just three or four audiobooks a year and dislike subscriptions.

Kobo Plus (around $9.99 per month) is the closest thing to an all-you-can-listen subscription: unlimited listening from a large catalog that skews toward backlist and indie titles. For voracious readers of romance, mystery, and sci-fi series, it is outstanding value.

Google Play Books works like Apple Books — individual purchases, no subscription, frequent aggressive sales — with the advantage of working identically on Android, iOS, and the web. Chirp needs no subscription at all: it sells limited-time deals on premium titles, often between $0.99 and $7.99. Smart listeners use Chirp as a supplement, grabbing deals on books they want to own while getting bulk listening from a subscription or Libby.

Our Verdict: Which App Should You Choose?

Match the app to your listening volume and budget:

  • 4+ books a month: Audible Premium Plus (credits for new releases you want to own) paired with Libby (free backlist). This combo beats every single-service option.
  • 2 books a month: Audible if you want ownership; Kobo Plus if you read mostly genre fiction and do not care about owning.
  • 1 book a month: Spotify Premium if you already subscribe for music; otherwise Libby plus occasional Chirp deals.
  • A few books a year: Skip subscriptions entirely — buy on sale from Apple Books, Google Play Books, or Chirp.
  • Tightest budget: Libby alone. A library card is the single highest-value "subscription" in audiobooks.

Many serious listeners end up with two services — typically Libby plus one paid option — and that hybrid approach is usually cheaper than forcing everything through a single app. To see the exact numbers for your habits, try the Audiobook Price Calculator, and read our deeper cost breakdown in the audiobook cost guide.

Summary

Audible wins on catalog and ownership, Spotify wins on bundled convenience, Libby wins on price (free), and the a la carte stores win for occasional listeners. Count how many books you actually finish per month, decide whether ownership matters to you, and pick the model — not the brand — that fits. Start with Libby regardless: it costs nothing and might cover half your reading list on its own.
